## The Blunt Verdict: This cannot be validly tested as a predictive model.
If I am staking my reputation, my honest conclusion is that you cannot validly test or train a statistical predictive model with fewer than 10 clean collapse cases and a 2-year data depth.
Any machine learning model, logistic regression, or statistical scoring rule built on this dataset will be an exercise in overfitting to noise. With fewer than 10 events, a single anomalous company (e.g., a firm where an auditor resigned simply because they merged with another accounting firm) will completely distort your feature weights. You do not have an experiment; you have a collection of case studies.
If you show a backtest with high precision or recall using this data, a skeptical quantitative reviewer will instantly reject it as a retrospective artifact of look-ahead bias and sample enrichment.

## What to Tell the Customer Instead (The Value Pivot)
Do not sell them an "AI-driven predictive model" or an "early-warning score." They will run it against their portfolio, watch it fail or generate endless false alarms, and cancel the subscription. [1]

"We do not offer a machine learning model that predicts bankruptcy two years out. With the current state of Indian private company data, anyone claiming to do that is selling you look-ahead bias. Instead, we offer a zero-lag compliance and friction monitor. The moment a key management personnel exits, a result is delayed past the statutory limit, or an auditor steps down, we pull the raw filing, parse the hidden context, and alert your risk team within 24 hours—months before it hits credit rating reports or the NCLT."

Risk teams will absolutely pay for a clean, automated feed that saves them from manually checking the MCA registry for all their mid-corporate borrowers.

## The One Valid Test: The "Friction-to-Default" Forensic Audit
If you must run a test over the next two weeks to prove to yourself and your early clients that these flags have any concurrent validity, you cannot use a forward-looking predictive design. You must run a Forensic Association Test.
This is the exact protocol to hand to an engineer tomorrow. It uses only your existing data, requires zero future assumptions, and directly checks if your flags are structurally linked to failure. [2] 
## 1. Universe & Sampling

* The Universe: Take your < 10 clean collapse cases.
* The Comparison Group: Pull a random sample of 100 active, surviving companies from the exact same sectors and asset-size brackets as your cases. Do not attempt to match them on financial distress.

## 2. The Clock (The Event-Horizon Lock)
Because the NCLT admission date lags behind reality, your engineer must discard it as a time anchor. Instead, execute an Event-Horizon Lock:

* For each of your < 10 cases, look up the Filing Date of the insolvency petition (not the admission date). Let this calendar date be $T_{\text{fail}}$.
* For the 100 surviving companies, assign them a dummy anchor date, $T_{\text{dummy}}$, distributed randomly across the same calendar months as your cases' $T_{\text{fail}}$.
* The Data Cutoff: For every company, your engineer must programmatically delete all filing data that occurred after its respective $T_{\text{fail}}$ or $T_{\text{dummy}}$.

## 3. Features (The Clean-Signal Matrix)
Because your data is coarse, you must strip out all volume-heavy and ambiguous flags. Move forward with only three high-integrity indicators:

   1. Auditor Resignation: Count of Form ADT-3 filings in the 12 months prior to the anchor date.
   2. KMP Churn: Count of Form DIR-12 filings showing the cessation of a CFO, Company Secretary, or Managing Director in the 12 months prior to the anchor date.
   3. Filing Latency: The number of days between the company's statutory financial year-end and the actual upload date of its annual returns (Form AOC-4).

## 4. The Statistical Metric: Fisher’s Exact Test
Do not calculate Precision, Recall, or ROC-AUC. With fewer than 10 cases, those metrics are meaningless. Instead, construct a simple $2 \times 2$ contingency table for each flag:

| | Triggered Flag (Yes) | Did Not Trigger Flag (No) |
|---|---|---|
| Collapsed Cases (< 10) | $A$ | $B$ |
| Surviving Cohort (100) | $C$ | $D$ |

Run Fisher's Exact Test on this matrix. This is the exact non-parametric test designed for small sample sizes where expected cell counts are below 5.
## 5. The Hard Pass/Fail Bar

* PASS (The Signal is Real): The Fisher’s Exact Test yields a $p$-value $< 0.05$. This mathematically proves that the concentration of these flags in your collapsing companies is not a random coincidence, justifying the build of an automated alert pipeline.
* FAIL (Kill the Project): The $p$-value $\ge 0.05$. This means that healthy, small Indian private companies exhibit these exact same friction points (sloppy filings, changing accounting firms, turning over CS personnel) just as frequently as failing ones. Your flags are simply background noise.

------------------------------
## The Residual Risk This Design Can't Beat
Even if this test passes perfectly, it cannot prove Temporal Precedence.
Because your data is only 2 years deep, you cannot definitively prove whether the auditor resigned before the company's bank accounts froze, or if the auditor resigned because the bank accounts froze and the promoter stopped paying their fees. If it is the latter, your feed is still a lagging indicator of a crisis that the company’s primary lenders already know about.
